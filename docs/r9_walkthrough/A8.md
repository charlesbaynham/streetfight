(hat purple) / `A8-Blue` (hat red), six players. Five drove the real
`/outfit_options` + `/pick_outfit` endpoints and hold picked outfits:

| player | tshirt | trousers | hat | armbands |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A8-Ada | green | mustard | purple | blue |
| A8-Bo | red | olive | purple | green |
| A8-Cy | yellow | black | purple | red |
| A8-Dee | yellow | red | red | black |
| A8-Eli | purple | olive | red | yellow |

A sixth, `A8-Fay`, was put on a team **without** picking, to reach the "no

**Every verdict below came from the stub vision model** (`$S/stub_openrouter.py`,
steered under `h.withStub` with `holder: "A8"`). The stub fabricates a
schema-conforming reading and never looks at the image — the green square in
every screenshot is the fake camera. So what is demonstrated is that the page
renders and scores *a* reading correctly, not that any identification is right.
The deterministic half (`shot_identification.rank_reference_candidates` →
`identity/decoder.py`) is real code and its numbers were cross-checked against a
standalone run of `decode()` on the same five words; they agree.

The core promises hold:

- **Nothing lands in the shot queue.** Across 9 captures and 2 re-reviews,
  `admin_get_shots_info` grew from 14 to 29 shots, and **not one of the 29
  belongs to me** — each was fetched with `admin_get_shot` and every game id /
  shooter name is another agent's (A7, A6, A11, A12, A4). Zero shots exist in
  game `b4836e37…` or for any `A8-*` user. The photo is on the `User`
  (`admin_get_reference_photo` returns a 30 KB `data:image/jpeg;base64,…`).
- **The "unreadable identifies nobody" case is handled correctly** — this is
  the R7 amendment working. `readable_channels: 0` produces
  `"ranked": [], "matches_expected": null` from the backend and the page prints
  a plain-English retake instruction in amber, with no name and no probability.
  It does *not* fall through to a confident-looking answer.
- **Amber is genuinely used for uncertainty, and every status is a word.**
  Green appears only for a confident correct match, red only for a confident
  mismatch (and for a failed review, see below). No status anywhere is a bare
  colour or icon; the tick/cross glyphs in the roster are always accompanied by
  text.
- Re-capture over an existing photo, `Re-run review`, and
  `admin_delete_reference_photo` all work from the UI; delete returns the roster
  row to "No photo yet" and clears the stored review
  (`admin_get_reference_photo` → 404). SSE live-updates the roster: deleting a
  photo from a second admin session changed the open page's row within seconds
  (`$S/shots/a8_24_sse_check.png`).

**On the "few readable channels" worry in the checklist.** With `armbands` and
`hat` erased and only Ada's t-shirt+trousers read, the page said, confidently
and in red, "Reads as A8-Ada (p=1.00), not A8-Eli". I do **not** think that is
the MDS pathology: this page scores against the five *actual* placed outfits,
not against the codebook, so with five players two channels really do
discriminate (p=0.9992, confirmed independently). The pathology belongs to
`shot_vision.slot_candidates_from_review`, which this path does not use. It is
still worth knowing that the big banner carries no hint that half the garments
were unseen — see the finding below.

### Finding: a reading that fits nobody still headlines "Probably <the player you photographed>"
**Severity:** minor
**What happens:** a confident four-channel reading of colours no player is
wearing (black / grey / orange / orange) makes the posterior degenerate — the
likelihood is zero for every candidate, so `decode()` falls back to the flat
prior and hands back p=0.20 for all five. `IdentificationVerdict` sees
`matches_expected === true` (the tie happens to put the photographed player
first) and prints, in bold, **"Probably A8-Ada (p=0.20), but not clearly enough
to call it."** The tone is amber and the flag line does say "the colours fit no
outfit cleanly", but the headline — the one thing read at the door — names the
right person on evidence that fits nobody. p=0.20 is exactly 1/5, the same
"prior handed straight back" tell that R7's amendment already fixed for
`readable_channels == 0`; the analogous "the reading explains nobody" case has
no equivalent guard.
**How to reproduce:** photograph any player with the stub set to
`{channels: {tshirt: ["black",0.92], trousers: ["grey",0.92], hat:
["orange",0.92], armbands: ["orange",0.92]}}`. See
`$S/shots/a8_14_verdict_matches_nobody.png`.
**Where:** `react-ui/src/ReferencePhotos.js:104-149` (`IdentificationVerdict`);
the backend flag is `inconsistent` in `backend/reference_photos.py:_identification`.
**Why it matters for the 19th:** somebody turning up in the wrong colours is the
exact failure the kit check exists to catch, and it is the case where the
headline is least helpful. An admin skimming amber banners could read
"Probably A8-Ada" as "fine, just a bit unsure" and wave them through.

### Finding: when the player has not picked an outfit, the page hides the one candidate that matters
**Severity:** minor
**What happens:** photographing `A8-Fay` (on a team, no slot) with a reading
that is *exactly* A8-Ada's outfit gives amber "A8-Fay has not picked an outfit,
so this photo cannot be checked against them." Underneath it lists
`A8-Bo (p=0.00)`, `A8-Cy (p=0.00)`, `A8-Dee (p=0.00)` — because the runners-up
are always `ranked.slice(1, 4)`, and in this branch the verdict text never
mentioned `ranked[0]`. The top match, **A8-Ada at p=1.00**, is the only line
worth showing and is the only one dropped.
**How to reproduce:** `$S/shots/a8_27_no_outfit_picked.png`; the stored payload
has `ranked[0] = A8-Ada, p=0.99999`.
**Where:** `react-ui/src/ReferencePhotos.js:122` (default text) and `:156`
(`ranked.slice(1, 4)`).
**Why it matters for the 19th:** "this person hasn't picked, and by the way they
photograph as A8-Ada" is a collision to fix at the door. As it stands the admin
sees three zeroes.

### Finding: the review's loading state claims the vision model is not configured
**Severity:** minor
**What happens:** `ReviewView` renders `state == null` as
**"Not reviewed - no vision model is configured."** `state` is also null while
`admin_get_reference_review` is in flight, so on a slow link opening any player
shows that sentence under their stored photo before the real verdict arrives.
It is a factual claim about the deployment, not a spinner — and the photo right
above it correctly says "Loading the stored photo...".
**How to reproduce:** deterministic with a 6 s delay routed onto
`admin_get_reference_review` — `$S/shots/a8_31_loading_says_not_configured.png`.
It also happened spontaneously once on the dev server under load
(`$S/a8/s15.js` output).
**Where:** `react-ui/src/ReferencePhotos.js:168-173`.
**Why it matters for the 19th:** the door is the moment an admin decides whether
CharlesBot is working. A page that says "no vision model is configured" on a
laggy phone will send someone off to debug an env var mid-queue.

### Finding: the game picker forces the page to scroll sideways on a phone
**Severity:** minor
**What happens:** `GameSelector`'s `<select>` labels each game as
`"<uuid8> (team, team, team, …)"`. The sample game lists ten team names, so the
select sizes itself to **769 px** on a 390 px viewport and
`document.documentElement.scrollWidth` is 778 vs a client width of 390 — the
whole exemplar page scrolls horizontally, and the selected value is clipped at
the right edge. Hiding the select alone restores `scrollWidth = 390`.
**How to reproduce:** open `/admin/reference` with more than one game in the DB;
measured in `$S/a8/s7.js`. Visible in `$S/shots/a8_02_roster_mygame.png`.
**Where:** `react-ui/src/ReferencePhotos.js:376-397` — the select has no CSS
class and `ReferencePhotos.module.css` gives it no `max-width`.
**Why it matters for the 19th:** on the night there will probably be one game
and `GameSelector` returns null, so this may never bite. But test games survive
in the DB, and a sideways-scrolling page is exactly the wrong thing on the one
page that is meant to be driven one-handed.

### Finding: `* { font-size: 12px }` shrinks the exemplar's own touch targets
**Severity:** minor
**What happens:** `react-ui/src/index.css:14-17` sets `font-size: 12px` on `*`,
so every `em` in `ReferencePhotos.module.css` resolves against 12 px (13.2 px
inside `.bigButton`, which sets `font-size: 1.1em`). Measured with
`boundingBox()` at 390x844:

| element | CSS | resolves to | rendered height |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `.bigButton` "Photograph X" | `min-height: 3.5em` | 46.2 px | 46.2 px |
| `.buttonRow button` (Re-run / Delete) | `min-height: 3em` | 36 px | 52.8 px (label wraps) |
| `.rosterRow` | `min-height: 3.2em` | 38.4 px | 50.1 px (content) |
| `.backButton` "← Roster" | `min-height: 2.6em` | **31.2 px** | **31.2 px** |

The roster rows and the capture button still clear 44 px on content and padding,
so the page is usable. The **back button does not** — 31 px, and it is the
control pressed once per player. The row also renders unevenly: "Retake" is
39.6 px next to two 52.8 px siblings, because `.buttonRow button` (0,1,1) beats
`.bigButton` (0,1,0) on the min-height.
**Where:** `react-ui/src/index.css:14-17`;
`react-ui/src/ReferencePhotos.module.css:38, 110, 129, 144`.
**Why it matters for the 19th:** CLAUDE.md names this file as the house style
and quotes those `em` values as "comfortably past the 44px touch minimum". They
are not, at the app's actual base font — worth knowing before another admin page
is built by copying them.

### Finding: "Review failed" is red, where the page's own rule says amber
**Severity:** cosmetic
**What happens:** a vision-call failure renders `styles.statusBad` in the roster
and a red error box in the detail view, the same red used for "✗ Reads as
somebody else". The page's own comment says green and red are for answers and
amber is for everything the machine is not sure of; a 500 from the model is not
an answer about the player at all.
**How to reproduce:** stub `{fail: true}` + "Re-run review" —
`$S/shots/a8_17_verdict_error.png`, roster row in `$S/shots/a8_22_pending_roster.png`.
**Where:** `react-ui/src/ReferencePhotos.js:47`, `:177-182`.
**Why it matters for the 19th:** at a glance a red pill on the roster means "this
player's kit is wrong". "The API fell over" wearing the same colour will send an
admin to re-dress a player who is fine. (The failure itself is reported well —
no infinite spinner, and re-running with a working model recovers a full verdict:
`$S/shots/a8_29_error_recovered.png`.)

### Finding: a green "HIT" tag on a kit check
**Severity:** cosmetic
**What happens:** `outcomeTag(review)` is reused from the shot queue, so every
reference photo carries a green **HIT** pill — including under the amber
"No garment could be read in this photo" verdict, where it is the only green
thing on the page. "HIT" is a shot-adjudication word ("the photograph shows a
person") and means nothing at the door.
**Where:** `react-ui/src/ReferencePhotos.js:197` (`outcomeTag`), rendered from
`react-ui/src/ShotQueue.js:271`.
**Stub caveat:** the *value* is a stub artefact — the stub only overrides keys
named `hit`/`is_hit`/…, so the schema's `shot_hit_a_person` is always true and
the tag would say HIT whatever I set. The *presence* of a green shot-domain tag
on this page is real and independent of that.
**Why it matters for the 19th:** minor, but it is one green pill on a page whose
whole colour language is "green means yes".

### Finding: the roster gives no sense of how much of the door queue is done
**Severity:** question
**What happens:** rows are ordered by team then name, with no count and no
grouping of the unchecked. With five players that is fine
(`$S/shots/a8_15_roster_mixed.png`); with twenty-five arriving in a rush, "who
have I not done yet" means scanning every row for the grey "No photo yet" pill.
**Where:** `react-ui/src/ReferencePhotos.js:69-95` (`Roster`); the ordering comes
from `AdminInterface.get_reference_photo_status`
(`backend/admin_interface.py:798`, `order_by(Team.name, User.name)`).
**Why it matters for the 19th:** the stated job is one-handed with a box of
armbands. A "6 of 24 checked" line, or unchecked-first ordering, is the
difference between a glance and a scan. Raising it as a question rather than a
bug — it may simply not matter at the real headcount.
